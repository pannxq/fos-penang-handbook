1. Welcome to Penang

Welcome to Penang! As the Friends of Stonyhurst (FOS) parent community, we are absolutely thrilled to welcome you to our beautiful island. Moving to a new country or city is a massive adventure for any family. This guide is built by parents who have walked in your shoes, designed to help you settle in quickly so you can focus on enjoying your new life here.

1.1 Penang at a Glance

Known affectionately as the "Pearl of the Orient," Penang is a vibrant state located on the northwest coast of Peninsular Malaysia. It is celebrated globally for its rich history, incredible street food, diverse multicultural community, and beautiful landscapes. For expat and relocating families, Penang offers an exceptional high quality of life with modern amenities, world-class healthcare, international schools, and a warm, welcoming community.

Penang is divided into two distinct regions connected by two major bridge systems: Penang Island and Seberang Perai (the mainland).

Penang Island (The Island): This is where Stonyhurst International School, Straits Quay and most expat residential areas are located. Families primarily cluster in the northeast coastal suburbs, including Tanjung Tokong, Tanjung Bungah, Gurney Drive, and Batu Ferringhi. Most families spend nearly all of their day-to-day life here.

Seberang Perai (The Mainland): Known as an industrial and manufacturing hub with vibrant local towns. Due to the daily commute across the bridge, it is less common for Stonyhurst families to reside on the mainland.

1.3 Weather & Climate

Penang enjoys a tropical rainforest climate, keeping the island warm, humid, and sunny year-round.

Temperatures: Typically hover between 28°C and 32°C during the day.

Rainfall Patterns: While there are no traditional four seasons, rainfall increases during the monsoon periods (typically April–May and September–November). Rain usually manifests as brief, heavy afternoon downpours rather than all-day drizzle.

💡 FOS Tip: Always keep a compact umbrella in your bag, and an extra one in the car. Lightweight, breathable cotton clothing and daily sunscreen are island essentials.

1.4 Languages & Communication

Malaysia is incredibly multilingual.

English: Extremely widely spoken across Penang, especially in commercial hubs, medical facilities, restaurants, and residential areas. You will have no trouble getting around with English.

Bahasa Melayu: The official national language. Learning a few simple terms – such as "Terima kasih" (thank you) – is always appreciated by locals!

Mandarin, Penang Hokkien, & Tamil: Also widely spoken across the island. Penang Hokkien is a unique local dialect that you will frequently hear at local hawker stalls and markets, even amongst our local Stonyhurst parents/ community.

1.5 Currency & Everyday Payments

The currency is the Malaysian Ringgit (MYR, often symbolized as RM).

Digital/QR Payments: Malaysia has rapidly transitioned to a cashless society. Almost all merchants, from high-end malls to roadside fruit stalls, accept DuitNow QR (cashless scanning linked to local bank accounts) or Touch 'n Go eWallet.

Credit Cards: Widely accepted in supermarkets, malls, and restaurants.

Cash: Still useful to keep a small amount of cash (RM 50-100), notes and coins for traditional wet markets, parking machines, and small hawker stalls.

1.6 Local Culture & Lifestyle

Penang is a vibrant melting pot of Malay, Chinese, Indian, and international cultures. This diversity shapes daily life on the island and is celebrated throughout the year through major cultural festivals – including Eid (Hari Raya), Chinese New Year, Deepavali and Christmas – alongside local public holidays.

Embracing this multicultural blend, along with Penang's world-famous culinary scene and warm hospitality, is one of the most rewarding parts of making the island your home.

Lifestyle: Very laid-back and family-oriented. Daily life revolves around food, family outings, outdoor activities, and community activities.

Cultural Etiquette: Always remove your shoes before entering someone’s home or a religious site. Dress modestly when visiting government offices or places of worship (shoulders and knees should be covered).

1.7 What New Families Should Know Before Arriving

Pack Wisely: Penang enjoys warm, tropical weather year-round. Focus on light summer clothing, swimwear, and comfortable walking shoes. Heavy winter gear is unnecessary unless you plan to travel internationally.

Tech Readiness: Malaysia uses UK-style Type G three-pin plugs (240V). If your electronics use different plug shapes, be sure to pack a few universal power adapters to use upon arrival.

Medical Prep: If anyone in your family requires specialized prescription medication, bring a multi-month supply alongside an official doctor’s letter. Local pharmacies in Malaysia may carry different brand names or operate under different dispensing regulations.
