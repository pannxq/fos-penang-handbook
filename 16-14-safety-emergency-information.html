14. Safety & Emergency Information

14.1 Emergency Numbers

Keep these vital emergency contact numbers saved in all family mobile phones:

National Emergency Hotline (Police, Ambulance, Fire & Rescue): Dial 999 (accessible from any landline or mobile).

Tourist Police Hotline: 04-269 4522 (dedicated assistance for international visitors and expats).

14.2 Hospitals & Emergency Medical Care

For urgent medical situations, Penang's leading private hospital Emergency Rooms provide 24/7 care with English-speaking medical staff:

Penang Adventist Hospital (Tanjung Tokong): 04-222 7200 (Closest emergency facility to Stonyhurst and surrounding expat neighborhoods).

Gleneagles Hospital Penang (Gurney Drive): 04-222 9111 (Premier private medical centre with comprehensive paediatric emergency facilities).

Island Hospital (George Town): 04-238 3333 (Fully equipped specialist emergency department).

FOS Tip: Program these direct emergency numbers into your phone contacts so domestic helpers, drivers, and older children can call immediately without hesitation.

14.3 Police & Fire Services

For local station inquiries or non-emergency assistance, contact your area stations directly:

Balai Polis Tanjung Tokong: 04-899 3222

Fire & Rescue (Bomba) Jalan Perak Station: 04-226 4444

14.4 Road & Personal Safety

Penang is generally very safe for families, but staying mindful of basic precautions ensures peace of mind:

Pedestrian Safety: Footpaths can be uneven, elevated, or abruptly end. Walk defensively, pay close attention when crossing busy roads, and look out for unexpected motorcyclists.

Snatch Theft Prevention: Carry handbags, backpacks, and valuables on the side away from oncoming road traffic to protect against opportunistic passing motorcyclists.

14.5 Scams & Fraud Prevention

Digital phone scams and impersonation attempts are increasingly prevalent globally. Common local tactics include unsolicited WhatsApp calls or SMS messages claiming to be from courier companies (e. g., Pos Malaysia), tax authorities (LHDN), or local banks.

Never share One-Time Passwords (OTPs), personal banking credentials, or transfer funds based on phone instructions. Official Malaysian authorities and financial institutions will never request payments or passwords via unofficial phone calls or messaging apps.

14.6 Children’s Safety

Keep children within visual range in crowded shopping complexes, festivals, and public spaces.

For water safety, ensure continuous adult supervision around condominium swimming pools and public beaches. When visiting beaches in Batu Ferringhi, be cautious of seasonal jellyfish warnings and strong coastal undercurrents.

14.7 Lost Passport, Bank Card or Phone

If a passport is lost or stolen, file an official police report at the nearest police station immediately. Contact your home country's Embassy or High Commission (many maintain diplomatic offices in Penang, while others operate in Kuala Lumpur) to apply for emergency travel documents.

If bank cards or mobile devices are lost, block card access immediately using your mobile banking application or by calling your bank's 24-hour card cancellation hotline.

14.8 Emergency Contacts for Families

To ensure quick response during an emergency, prepare a written emergency card displayed prominently at home containing your complete home address written in both English and Malay (crucial for directing ambulance operators), landlord contact details, consular hotlines, and Stonyhurst reception contacts.

At a Glance: Provides immediate reference for emergency situations, listing emergency phone numbers, 24/7 private ER services, local police stations, pedestrian and child safety guidance, scam awareness, and procedures for lost passports or bank cards.
